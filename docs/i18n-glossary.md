# Glazelab glossary (UI languages)

**Machine translation, needs native review.** The Italian (`it`, standard Italian) and Spanish (`es`, neutral Spanish) dictionaries were written by an AI translator and checked only by scripts (same keys, placeholders and ICU plural structure as `en.json`) and by a second AI pass over the most visible texts. No native speaker or lawyer has reviewed them. The German and Portuguese columns are what `de.json` and `pt-PT.json` say today, for comparison. The translated terms of service say above their text that the English version binds (`terms.translationNote`).

Rules for every language: never translate user content, brand names, product names or "Glazelab"; tags are lower case and hyphen-joined with accents kept (`lib/tags/tags.ts`).

## Register and tone

- Italian: informal "tu" throughout, like the German "du"; gendered adjectives about the person avoided where a neutral form exists ("Eccoti di nuovo", "Uscita effettuata"); plain `'` for elisions.
- Spanish: neutral Spanish, "tú", never "vosotros", "ustedes" avoided, no words of one region where a shared one exists ("tomar foto", "dispositivo", "teléfono"; never "móvil", "celular", "ordenador" or "computadora"); angle quotes «…»; no ASCII apostrophe.
- Both: the plural categories of `Intl.PluralRules` (`one`, `many`, `other`); the messages give `one` and `other`, and the "many" of millions falls back to `other`.

## Decisions of the naturalness review

A best-effort read by a model, not a native speaker; the goal was "does not sound weird". A native read stays welcome but is not needed (maintainers' decision). The open taste questions of the earlier review are settled as follows.

- Italian:
  - library = "libreria" (established app usage; "raccolta" would touch about 20 keys for no gain; "biblioteca" is for books).
  - jar size = "formato del barattolo", never "dimensione".
  - similar looks = "Foto simili".
  - the Mine chip = "Le mie" (also where notices name it).
  - glazes are "sullo scaffale", never "nello".
  - the hobby terms keep "È offerto in base alla disponibilità" for "provided as available"; never "così com'è" (that would mean "as is", a promise the English does not make).
  - the terms' account section is "Il tuo account e chi contattare", so it is not read as the buddies section ("contatti").
  - "Occorrenze" for times seen, "Schema colori" for colour scheme, plain apostrophes in every key.
  - Left for a native reader: "il tuo studio degli smalti" (alternative "laboratorio"), "Più amati" (alternative "Più salvati"), the stiff `errors.clubAdminRequired` (a test pins its text).
- Spanish:
  - report (photo, profile picture) = "denunciar" / "denuncia" ("reportar" is the Latin American everyday word, but "denunciar" is what the big platforms use in both regions).
  - an account ban = "expulsión" / "expulsar"; a blocked address or picture = "bloqueo" / "bloquear" (the two stay apart).
  - method chips "Pistola" and "Pera" stay (alternatives "Pulverizado", "Perilla" (a doorknob in Latin America), "Trazado").
  - the status chip is "Esperando fotos".
  - sign-in is "Iniciar sesión" everywhere; only the header's short button keeps "Entrar / registrarse" (it must fit 320 px).
  - "escribiste" and "has escrito" both stay as the sentence has them (simple past for what the user typed, compound past for recent events).
  - the hobby terms keep "Se ofrece según su disponibilidad".
  - buttons, menus and action titles use the infinitive ("Registrar una combinación"); headings that instruct the user as a step use the imperative ("Revisa tu correo").
  - neutral wording: "escribe/completa" not "introduce/rellena", "presiona" not "pulsa", "conectar con el servidor" not "contactar", "como máximo".
  - Left for a native reader: "Un proyecto personal" for "A hobby project", "placa" for the kiln shelf, "ratón" (Latin America: "mouse"), the isolated chip "Mías".
